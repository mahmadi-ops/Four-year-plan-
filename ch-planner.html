    Section 1 Mathematics four-year planner

    Students build their own plan. Set the next quarter to plan, then use “+ add course” to put each course taken at Santa Clara in the quarter it was taken, and each course you intend to take in a later quarter. AP (Advanced Placement), IB (International Baccalaureate), or transfer credit is ticked separately under “Credit from AP, IB or transfer” and does not appear in the plan. Once a course has been added, a “Sample four-year plan” appears below the plan: the student’s own courses in grey and suggested courses for every remaining quarter, following the department’s sample curriculum for the major (Fall 2025) and respecting every prerequisite and co-requisite in the Bulletin, the quarters each course is offered, and the alternate-year rotation. The “+” on a suggestion copies it into the plan. The requirement check marks what is completed and what is in the plan. For comfortable use, open the planner full size in a new tab.
    🔗

    Important.

    A plan made here is a draft. Discuss it with your advisor and go over it together in an advising session before you finalize it.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1 The planner

    Instructions.

    Entries are kept in the browser, so an advisor can set up a student, close the page, and come back to it. “Clear student” resets everything except the offerings table. “Download plan (PDF)” opens the browser’s print dialog with only the plan on the page: a header naming the emphasis and the completed courses, the four year-tables, the warnings, and the requirement check. Choose “Save as PDF” there to keep a copy. A plan is kept in the browser it was made in; “Save or share this plan” gives a short code (also printed on the PDF) and, on the live site, a link, either of which restores the plan anywhere, so a student can bring it to an advising meeting or send it ahead. Above the plan, a line totals the major units in it and the upper-division units, against the university’s 175 and 60. Core courses and courses the menus do not list can be added with “+ Type a course…”. An upper-division MATH course numbered 101 to 189 added this way counts toward the seven upper-division courses (MATH 100 and courses numbered 190 and above do not), as do the alternatives the Bulletin names for an emphasis, such as ECON 174 in Data Science or MATH 8 in Mathematics Education; the planner does not check prerequisites or offering quarters for typed-in courses.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.2 How the plan is built

    The planner starts from the set of courses the student still needs: the lower-division core, the emphasis courses, one course from each required upper-division category, any electives the student asked for, and enough further electives to reach seven upper-division courses. For each of these it computes the latest workable quarter: the last quarter in which the course is offered that still leaves room for everything that depends on it. A course whose latest workable quarter has already passed is reported as blocked, with the chain that caused it.
    🔗
    It then walks forward from the next quarter to plan. In each quarter it takes the courses whose prerequisites are met, that are offered that quarter, and that fit under the per-quarter cap, choosing the most urgent first: earliest latest-workable-quarter, then alternate-year courses, then lower-division courses.
    🔗
    Three kinds of warning appear under the tables.
    🔗
    Blocked
    🔗
    A course can no longer be completed by Year 4 Spring, either because an alternate-year course has no remaining offering or because a chain such as MATH 52 \(\to\) MATH 111 \(\to\) MATH 112 has run out of quarters.
    🔗
    Cannot wait
    🔗
    A course within the next year that has no later slot in the plan, with the reason: the courses that must follow it, or the fact that it is not offered again before graduation.
    🔗
    Note
    🔗
    Advice that does not change the plan, such as the deadline for declaring an emphasis (end of junior year) and the fact that MATH 195 satisfies the ELSJ requirement but does not count toward the seven upper-division courses.
    🔗
    The plan shows only major courses. Quarters marked “core / electives” have no major course scheduled and are where Core Curriculum and free electives go.

    Subsection 1.3 Course offerings

    Where the quarters for each course come from:
    🔗
    • Upper-division MATH and CSCI courses, including the alternate-year rotation: the department’s upper-division course schedule (revised May 2026).
      🔗
      🔗
    • MATH 51, 52 and 53: the tentative 2026–27 list in the department’s CS advising document.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Calculus (MATH 11–14) every quarter, MATH 23 every Winter, CSCI 10 in Fall and Winter, and CSCI 60, 61 and 62 every quarter: confirmed by the department (October 2026).
      🔗
      🔗
    • PHYS 31 (Winter, Spring) and PHYS 32 (Fall, Spring): the Physics department’s class schedule, 2023–24 through Fall 2026.
      🔗
      🔗
    • CSEN 178 and the business and economics courses of the emphases: the University’s class listings for 2023–24 through Fall 2026, the same listings shown on the department class schedules (for example the Physics class schedule).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Offered by request only (MATH 100, 133, 134, 172): no quarters, and never suggested.
      🔗
      🔗
    Any entry can be changed in the offerings table at the bottom of the planner; edits persist in the browser only, so lasting changes go in the course table near the top of assets/planner.html.
